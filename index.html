01 / Lore & chronology

从冰封中醒来
再走进两个时代

距今约一万年前，锡尔维德是一名天生的冰原猎手。天气突变的那一次狩猎中，他跌入裂开的冰川，身体与年龄一同被寒冷按下暂停。

冰层替他保存了生命，也替他错过了一个物种消失的时代。再次睁眼时，熟悉的猎场已经沉入传说。风从雪线上吹过，也灌进高楼的缝隙。

他仍然相信速度，仍然相信刀刃划开空气时那一瞬间的轻响。冰系法术似乎与他同源，但比起让世界冻结，他更喜欢让自己先一步抵达。

“两块猎场，一个猎人，一把刀。”

Hrosgaard / Northern Frontier

远古冰原的守望者

赫罗斯加德没有真正安静过。风从山口压下来，卷过结冰的湖面和被雪埋去一半的旧路，把远处的狼嚎磨得很薄。村民说，越过最后一座烽火台之后，林海里住着一只白色剑齿虎。他很少进入村庄，也不接受雇佣，只在暴雪封路、猎物失踪，或有人在冰层上发现不属于这个时代的足迹时出现。

锡尔维德习惯在天亮前出发。冰原猎人的服装并不华丽，银灰色的绒毛压在皮革和金属之间，天蓝色的纹路在雪光里一闪即逝。他沿着风向判断天气，凭兽迹辨认昨夜经过的东西；有时他会停在冰裂旁，低头听很久，像是在等冰层下某种微弱的回应。

沃尔德赫姆跟在他身后。那是一个高大的蓝色灵体，戴着银色面具，手里握着比人还高的横刀。它从不说话，也从不解释自己的选择。真正需要回答时，面具下只会传来一声低沉的吼，短促，像远雷滚过冰原。锡尔维德已经学会分辨其中的意思。

“今天不追。”他会对沃尔德赫姆说，“风太乱，留下的脚印不是真的。”

灵体低吼一声，刀尖擦过雪面，留下一道蓝色的痕。锡尔维德看了它一眼，伸手把刀柄往回按了半寸。“我知道你看见了。但我们不是来替旧时代收尸的。”

锡尔维德继续向北。赫罗斯加德的边境正在变冷，猎人的营火越来越少，巡逻队也不再愿意走进林海深处。偶尔有人在山脊上看见一道白色身影。第二天，失踪的牲畜会回到围栏，冰面上的裂缝被标出，某些不该靠近村庄的东西则重新退回雪线之外。

锡尔维德从不留下名字。他只在旧木桩上刻下一道横线，提醒后来的人这里曾经有人守护过。沃尔德赫姆站在风中，面具朝向更远的冰原，发出一声低吼。那不是命令，也不像警告，更像一位沉默的老战友，在确认他们仍然走在同一条路上。

Hrosgaard had never been truly quiet. Wind pressed down from the mountain passes, sweeping over frozen lakes and old roads half-buried in snow, thinning the wolves’ cries until they sounded far away. The villagers said that beyond the last beacon tower, a white sabretooth lived somewhere in the forest. He rarely entered the villages and never took paid work. He appeared only when a blizzard closed the roads, livestock went missing, or someone found tracks on the ice that did not belong to this age.

Silvade usually left before dawn. The clothing of an icefield hunter was practical rather than ornate: silver-gray fur pressed beneath leather and metal, sky-blue markings appearing and vanishing in the snowlight. He read the weather by the direction of the wind and identified the night’s visitors by their tracks. Sometimes he stopped beside a crack in the ice and listened for a long while, as if waiting for some faint answer from below.

Wyrdhelm followed behind him. It was a tall blue spirit, wearing a silver mask and carrying a long sword taller than a man. It never spoke and never explained its choices. When an answer was needed, only a low growl came from beneath the mask—brief and heavy, like distant thunder crossing the icefield. Silvade had learned to read the meaning in it.

“Not today,” he would say to Wyrdhelm. “The wind is too erratic. Those tracks are not real.”

The spirit would growl, its blade-tip brushing the snow and leaving a blue mark behind. Silvade would glance at it and press the hilt back by half an inch. “I know what you saw. But we are not here to bury the old world.”

Silvade continued north. The Hrosgaard border was growing colder, the hunters’ fires fewer, and the patrols less willing to enter the deep forest. From time to time, someone on a ridge saw a white figure. By morning, missing livestock would be back inside their pens, cracks in the ice would be marked, and certain things that should never have come near the villages would have retreated beyond the snowline.

Silvade never left his name behind. He only carved a single horizontal mark into an old wooden post, a reminder to those who came later that someone had once kept watch there. Wyrdhelm stood in the wind, its masked face turned toward the farther icefields, and gave a low growl. It was not an order, and it did not sound like a warning. It sounded more like a silent old comrade confirming that they were still walking the same road.

Helyon / Tiger City

未眠都市

赫利昂的雨从空轨与广告屏之间斜斜落下，在高架停车层的护栏上敲出细密的声响。战斗结束还不到二十分钟，地下维护通道里的焦味尚未散去；地面上的虎族都市却已经把警报和骚动折进夜色，照常亮起下一轮招牌。

锡尔维德坐在车前盖边缘，横刀横放在膝上。刀刃已经擦净，只有护手缝里留着一线雨水。他右臂的衣料被划开，伤口不深，胸腹上的天蓝纹路也在逐渐暗下去。这点伤不碍事，却足够提醒他今晚应该停一停。

对面的楼体正在播放广告，巨大的虎族模特隔着雨幕俯视街区。更高处，末班空轨从楼群间穿过，窗内挤满刚下班的乘客。赫利昂不会因为一场追逐停下来。它总能把破损、血迹和临时封锁藏进下一秒的灯光里，让一切看起来都只是城市运转时的杂音。

锡尔维德关掉终端上闪烁的定位界面。追他的人暂时不会再出现。他没有检查对方留下的数据片，也不想顺着线索去碰背后的公司、派系和账本。今晚的麻烦已经处理完了，剩下的可以等到天亮。

他把横刀放到一旁，摘下终端，重新检查伤口。雨水顺着耳侧的白毛滑下来，落在金属地面上。远处传来几声短促的警报，随后又归于平静。有人在替这座城市收拾残局，至于是谁，通常并不重要。

停车层下方的夜市重新支起遮棚，热油和姜茶的气味顺着楼梯间飘上来。锡尔维德收好刀，站到护栏边，看着霓虹在积水里断成细碎的蓝白色线条。赫利昂很大，大到足以让一只从冰川醒来的剑齿虎暂时不必解释自己从哪里来。

他在终端上写下一行简短记录：目标脱离，路线封存，明日再查。写完之后，他没有立刻离开，只是让自己在雨声里多坐了一会儿。战斗后的安静并不真正安静，通风机、车流、广告语和远处的脚步共同组成一层持续的背景噪音，却也足够让思绪慢慢恢复原位。

雨势渐缓，空轨驶过时带起的风吹散了最后一点焦味。锡尔维德拉紧外套，沿着仍在发光的街道走下去。今晚的休憩并不安稳，但已经足够。

Rain fell diagonally through the space between Helyon’s skyrails and advertising screens, tapping against the guardrails of an elevated parking deck. The fight had ended less than twenty minutes ago, and the smell of burning still lingered in the maintenance tunnel below. At street level, however, the tiger city had already folded the alarms and commotion into the night and moved on to its next set of illuminated signs.

Silvade sat on the edge of his car’s hood, his long sword resting across his knees. The blade had been wiped clean; only a thin line of rain remained in the seams of the guard. His right sleeve was torn, exposing a shallow wound, while the sky-blue markings across his torso were slowly dimming. It was nothing serious, but enough to tell him that he should stop for the night.

An advertisement played across the building opposite him, a giant tiger model looking down over the district through the rain. Higher above, the last skyrail of the night passed between the towers, its windows crowded with passengers just off their shifts. Helyon did not stop for a street pursuit. It simply hid damage, bloodstains, and temporary closures inside the next second of light, making them look like ordinary noise from a city in motion.

Silvade switched off the tracking display flashing across his terminal. The people who had followed him would not return for a while. He did not inspect the data shard they had left behind, nor did he intend to follow it into the companies, factions, and accounts behind them. Tonight’s trouble had been dealt with. The rest could wait until morning.

He set the long sword aside, removed the terminal, and checked the wound again. Rain slid from the white fur beside his ear onto the metal floor. A few short alarms sounded in the distance before the city settled again. Someone was cleaning up the aftermath. Who it was usually did not matter.

In the night market below, vendors raised their awnings again. The smell of hot oil and ginger tea drifted up through the stairwell. Silvade secured the sword and stood by the guardrail, watching neon break into thin blue-white lines across the puddles. Helyon was large enough that a sabretooth awakened from a glacier could put off explaining where he came from.

On his terminal, he typed a brief report: target lost, route sealed, investigate tomorrow. When he finished, he did not leave at once. He simply sat for a little longer beneath the rain. The quiet after a fight was never truly quiet; ventilation fans, traffic, advertisements, and distant footsteps formed a constant layer of noise. Still, it was enough to let his thoughts settle back into place.

The rain began to ease, and the wind of a passing skyrail carried away the last trace of burning. Silvade tightened his coat and walked on along the still-glowing street. Tonight’s rest was not a peaceful one, but it was enough.
